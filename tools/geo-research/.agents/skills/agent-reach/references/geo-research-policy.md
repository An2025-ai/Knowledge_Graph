# GEO Research Source and Citation Policy

Use this policy for China-focused GEO, AI search, brand marketing, market sizing,
competitor, customer-pain, investment, or industry-trend research.

## Mandatory workflow

1. Define the topic, entities, geography, decision audience, and current calendar year.
2. Search every applicable source group below. Use `site:DOMAIN KEYWORDS YEAR` queries
   through SearXNG, then open the original result page. Do not cite a search-result page.
3. Prefer material published in the current calendar year. If it is insufficient, expand
   to the preceding 24 months and label the date fallback. Use older sources only for
   definitions, regulation history, academic foundations, or long-term comparison.
4. Keep a coverage ledger with one row per required domain: `searched`, `used`,
   `no relevant result`, `login/paywall`, `blocked`, or `not applicable`.
5. Crawl or read the original page before using a claim. A snippet is discovery evidence,
   not report evidence. Preserve title, publisher, publication date, original URL,
   access status, quoted fact, and limitations.
6. Triangulate major conclusions across at least two independent source types when possible.
7. Put one or more clickable original URLs immediately after every factual conclusion,
   number, comparison, product capability, user quote, or market assertion.
8. End every report with both `搜索覆盖清单` and `完整信源清单`.

## Autonomous authoritative-source discovery

When the user does not know or provide authoritative sources, do not ask them to build the
source list. Discover appropriate domestic and international authorities before broad web
collection:

1. Search for the topic together with government statistics, regulator, ministry, official
   dataset, standard, industry association, research institute, university center, methodology,
   annual report, white paper, and the current year, in both Chinese and English.
2. Follow citations and bibliographies from credible reports to the original data owner or
   publisher. Prefer the original dataset/report over articles quoting it.
3. Verify a candidate authority before adopting it: legal/institutional identity, official
   domain ownership, named author or research team, publication date, methodology, sample/scope,
   funding or commercial relationship, and availability of the original report/data.
4. Do not treat high search ranking, media repetition, `.org`/`.com` suffixes, a polished report,
   or self-description as proof of authority.
5. For major market or user claims, seek one domestic primary/established source and one
   independent corroborating source where available. Explain when no suitable international or
   domestic counterpart exists.
6. Add newly discovered sources to the coverage ledger with discovery query, organization type,
   why it is authoritative, access status, whether it was used, and limitations.
7. Exclude lead-generation sites, copied data portals, unattributed charts, synthetic rankings,
   and reports whose original publisher or method cannot be verified.

This discovery protocol supplements the mandatory source list below; it never replaces or skips
the listed sources. Apply it to GEO and to every other industry/brand research profile.

### Zhihu and Xiaohongshu sampling rules

Apply these rules whenever Zhihu or Xiaohongshu is used to support user pain points,
purchase criteria, product requirements, brand demand, or competitor selection. These are
qualitative purposive-sampling minimums, not representative population samples.

| Platform | Candidate pool | Minimum effective posts | Minimum effective comments |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Zhihu | Inspect the first 20 results for each applicable query | 8-12 pages | 15 comments in total |
| Xiaohongshu | Inspect the first 20 results for each applicable query | 12-20 notes | 20 comments in total |

1. Use at least four query families covering: category/product terms, purchase or vendor
   selection, ROI/effect/attribution, and complaints/risks/avoidance. Add industry-specific
   queries when the research concerns a vertical market.
2. Count a post as effective only when its body or comment section contributes at least one
   concrete experience, business question, objection, counterexample, use case, evaluation
   criterion, or actionable requirement relevant to the research question.
3. Count a comment as effective only when it contains an actual experience, business context,
   causal explanation, explicit objection, counterexample, or actionable requirement. Do not
   count contact requests, resource requests, emojis, generic praise, vendor self-promotion,
   copied rankings, or apparent AI summaries.
4. Include different evidence roles rather than selecting only popular positive posts. The
   effective-post set must cover, where results exist: direct user/buyer questions, negative or
   failed experiences, practitioner explanations, and disagreement or counterexamples.
5. Do not rank candidates by engagement alone. Use engagement only to discover discussion-rich
   pages; prefer detailed low-engagement evidence over high-engagement promotional content.
6. Continue until the platform minimum is reached. Early stopping is allowed only after five
   consecutive inspected candidates add no new theme and all required evidence roles above have
   been covered. Record the five candidates and state that thematic saturation was used.
7. If login, CAPTCHA, deleted content, search quality, or insufficient relevant discussion
   prevents reaching the minimum, report the actual candidate count, effective-post count,
   effective-comment count, and blocker. Label the platform coverage `partial`; never silently
   lower the minimum or claim saturation.
8. In the report, disclose for each platform: search queries, sorting/filter state when visible,
   candidate results inspected, effective posts retained, effective comments retained, excluded
   content categories, stopping reason, and sampling limitations. Preserve an exact original URL
   for every retained post.

Do not apply these numeric sampling minimums to Knowledge Planet. Its access model, group-level
permissions, and member-comment availability vary too much for a comparable fixed quota. When it
is used, report groups accessed, topics opened, member comments actually visible, author
concentration, and access limitations without claiming representative coverage.

### C. Competitors and current events

For every material competitor, search and read:

- Official homepage, product page, pricing, documentation, case studies, newsroom/blog,
  public WeChat article, company registration/filing information where relevant.
- CAICT, iResearch, iFenxi, LeadLeo, 36Kr, Huxiu, and Morketing coverage.
- Funding, acquisition, customer, partnership, and product-release news from the original
  announcement plus a reputable independent report.

Separate facts into: official product facts, third-party observations, vendor-reported
cases, and independently verified outcomes. Never present an anonymous vendor case as an
independently verified customer result.

### E. Supplementary domestic and international sources

Use these only after the mandatory domestic groups, or where they provide unique evidence:

- Government/regulation: CAC, SAMR, MIIT, courts, official standards bodies.
- Academic: arXiv, ACM Digital Library, ACL Anthology, Google Scholar, CNKI, Wanfang.
- International research/product/user evidence: Gartner, Forrester, Similarweb,
  Statcounter, G2, Capterra, Product Hunt, Reddit, TechCrunch, vendor filings and official
  documentation.

## Evidence grades

- `A`: government statistics/regulation, peer-reviewed or primary academic evidence,
  official filings, named and reproducible primary datasets.
- `B`: established research institution or reputable reporting with disclosed method,
  sample, date, and scope.
- `C`: first-party product, pricing, announcement, or named corporate statement.
- `D`: vendor-reported anonymous case, community post, engagement signal, or claim without
  independent audit.
- `E`: unverifiable, misattributed, copied ranking, synthetic roundup, or claim without an
  original source. Exclude from conclusions and list it under rejected claims if material.

## Citation format

Follow the existing report style: place citations at the end of the sentence or bullet.
Each citation must be a clickable link to the exact original page, for example:

```markdown
截至 2025 年末，中国生成式 AI 用户规模达到 6.02 亿
[CNNIC，第57次统计报告](https://www.cnnic.cn/n4/2026/0304/c88-11549.html)。
```

For each source appendix row include:

`编号 | 层级 | 机构/作者 | 标题 | 日期 | 搜索查询 | 原始网址 | 访问状态 | 使用的事实 | 证据等级 | 局限`

For every major synthesis, state its basis explicitly:

```markdown
**判断依据：** 艾瑞用户调查 + 央视调查报道 + 3 家竞品官网功能交叉验证
[来源1](URL) [来源2](URL) [来源3](URL)。
```

## Quality gates

Do not finalize the report unless all applicable checks pass:

- Every mandatory source group has a coverage-ledger outcome.
- Current-year sources were searched explicitly.
- Major conclusions use direct original URLs and identify their source basis.
- All numbers include date, population/sample, scope, and methodology when available.
- Product pricing and features come from official pages; customer outcomes are correctly
  labeled by verification status.
- User pain points include actual user or investigative evidence, not only vendor marketing.
- Search snippets, copied rankings, and unverified market-size claims are not used as facts.
- The report lists searched domains even when no relevant result was found.
